Why a Tiggo 8 Pro enquiry collapses before anyone talks about price

A buyer writes "Tiggo 8 Pro brake pads" into a message, receives three quotations within a day, and discovers at the container that the three suppliers quoted three different numbers. Nothing was dishonest. The buyer gave a model name where the market sells several production specifications under that name, and every supplier silently guessed which one he meant. On this platform the guess is expensive because the pads and discs are split by axle, by running gear and by production period, and the cooling parts are split by whether the compressor is driven by a belt or by an electric motor. A wrong guess is only visible when the boxes are opened.

The catalogue behind this guide holds forty references for the Tiggo 8 Pro: twenty under Brake Parts and twenty under Cooling System. That even split is itself a useful clue. It means a generic "Chinese SUV parts" enquiry is already two enquiries, and the two halves need different evidence to be answered. A brake line needs the axle position and the state of the running gear; a cooling line needs the compressor architecture. Neither can be settled by a photograph of the car, and neither should be settled by the model name on the enquiry form.

What follows is the workflow we use at the counter: establish the vehicle variant, read the reference as an identifier rather than a description, sort the candidate into the right family, run a short physical verification, and only then attach commercial terms. Every OE number quoted below is taken from the catalogue table for this model. None of them is a promise that a part will fit a particular car or that it can be delivered from a shelf. Fitment must be confirmed against the VIN or the label on the original part before ordering.

Reading a Chery reference: prefixes, suffixes, and the variant gate

Chery references in this catalogue are compact and unforgiving. A 204 prefix belongs to the brake group; 301 and 302 prefixes belong to the cooling group; T1, T158 and J6 series appear alongside them as further distinct references. The nose of the number tells you which conversation you are in. The tail tells you which variant of that conversation. A suffix is not decoration: T158107350 and T158107350BA are two different catalogue lines, and the second one is not a "newer version of the same thing" until the label proves it.

The practical habit is to treat every character as significant and every family as a small population rather than a single part. The distribution below is counted from the forty catalogue rows, not estimated. It tells a buyer where depth exists and where a single number covers a wide run.

Six checks before the reference goes into a request for quotation

Identification work is repetitive, and that is precisely why it pays. Each of the steps below takes less time than one wrong shipment, and each one removes a specific class of error. Run them in order; skipping step three is the most common cause of a cooling part arriving with the wrong drive type.

  1. Step 1 — record the vehicle identity, not the marketing name. Take the VIN or the identification plate, the build date, the engine or powertrain code and the destination market. A Tiggo 8 Pro built for one market is not automatically the same specification as an export unit from a different year.
  2. Step 2 — fix the axle and side on brake lines. For every disc and pad line, state front or rear. For the wider pad population, also state whether the vehicle carries the standard or reinforced running gear, if that is visible on the documentation. Do not infer axle position from the photograph of a loose pad.
  3. Step 3 — establish the compressor architecture. Look at the vehicle, not the listing. A poly-V belt compressor such as 301000131AA or 301000711AA and an electric compressor such as 301000093AA or 301001882AA occupy different catalogue lines and use different connections. Write down what you actually see: belt driven, or electrical connector with a stated number of cavities.
  4. Step 4 — copy the reference character by character. Retain leading digits, the AA ending, the EP ending and the BA ending. T158107350 and T158107350BA are two lines. Retype nothing from memory; paste from the label or the old invoice.
  5. Step 5 — take one photograph that shows the label and the interface together. For brake parts, include the mounting face and the overall diameter. For cooling parts, include the connector and the pipe or hose positions. One useful frame is worth a page of description.
  6. Step 6 — keep identity and commerce in separate blocks. Put quantity, destination port, preferred shipping terms and documentation needs after the identification block. If a supplier proposes an alternative, file it as pending evidence and keep it off the accepted order list until the interfaces have been verified in writing.

One further discipline matters when the old part is no longer available to photograph. In that case, ask the supplier to state the basis for the proposed reference and to show the matching dimensions or interface photograph. An alternative supported by a dimension drawing is a candidate. An alternative supported only by the words "same model" is not.

How market and duty cycle change the mix

The same catalogue behaves differently in different regions, and a sensible first order reflects that. In hot markets, the cooling half does the work: evaporator cores, condensers and compressors see continuous load, and a vehicle parked outside in midsummer spends hours at a cabin temperature far above ambient. Buyers in those markets often ask about 301000058AA and J608105010 condensers, or about the electric compressor references 301000093AA and 301001882AA, long before they ask about pads. In dusty markets and on rough roads, the brake half dominates: front discs such as 204000081AA and pad sets such as T1C3501080 turn over faster per kilometre than the same parts do on a highway car. In colder regions the heater branch becomes relevant, and T158107130 heater cores and J608107110 heater motors appear in enquiries that a tropical buyer would never raise. The expansion tank 302001684AA is a small line that is easy to forget until a fleet needs it.

These patterns are planning observations, not measured wear rates, and they do not override the vehicle in front of you. They are useful for deciding which references to ask about first when you are building a trial mix. This is not distrust of the supplier; it is the only way to keep a forty-reference catalogue from collapsing into a handful of assumptions.

Questions buyers ask before the first order

Does 204000081AA fit every Tiggo 8 Pro front disc?

Do not assume so. It is one of four front disc references in the catalogue, alongside 204000453AA, 204001231AA and T153501075. Confirm against the VIN or the label on the original disc before ordering. The model name does not select between them.

Can I treat T1C3501080 as the same pad set as 204002030AA?

Not on the strength of the description alone. Both are listed as front brake pads, but they are separate catalogue references. Ask for the dimensional evidence behind any proposed cross-reference and keep the substitution pending until it arrives.

Why does the cooling quotation ask whether the compressor is electric?

Because the catalogue separates electric compressors such as 301000093AA and 301001882AA from belt-driven units such as 301000131AA and 301000711AA. The connector and the drive arrangement differ. Looking at the vehicle settles it in a minute; looking at a listing usually does not.

Two evaporator references differ only by a BA suffix. Does that matter?

Yes. T158107350 and T158107350BA are two catalogue lines. A suffix often indicates a revision in connection position, core depth or bracket. Photograph the label of the original part and copy the whole string, including the ending.

Are the forty catalogue references a list of parts you can deliver immediately?

No. The table is an identification aid for sourcing enquiries. Availability, lead time and documentation depend on the reference and the destination, and are confirmed against the specific order. Nothing in this guide should be read as a delivery or stock undertaking.

What should I send if I cannot read the old part number at all?

Send the VIN or plate, the build date, the destination market, the axle or subsystem, overall dimensions and a photograph of the part in position. That is usually enough to narrow the candidate list, but final confirmation still depends on matching the label or the physical interface against the proposed reference.
