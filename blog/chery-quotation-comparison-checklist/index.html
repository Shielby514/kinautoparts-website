Comparing Two Quotations for the Same Chery Reference

Published 24 November 2026 · KINP

Two suppliers can quote the same Chery reference at very different prices without either quote being wrong. Before you compare the numbers, check that both offers describe the same physical item under the same conditions: the complete reference, the vehicle variant, the quoted specification, the quantity basis and the delivery terms. A price is only meaningful once those fields match.

Start from the identified item, not the price

A quotation describes one item under a set of conditions. Two lines that show the same short number can still refer to different components if one carries a suffix the other dropped, or if one was priced for a different destination market. Ask each supplier to restate the full reference and the identification basis they used — the original label, the vehicle details, or a supplier catalogue — before placing the two prices side by side.

Align the full reference and vehicle variant

Copy the number exactly as it appears, including leading zeros, letters and version suffixes. Keep the model year, powertrain and destination market beside it. A model name such as Tiggo 8 Pro narrows the search but does not confirm that two offers cover the same part. If one supplier cannot say which variant they priced, treat that line as unconfirmed rather than as the cheaper option.

Align the fields that change the delivered cost

Put these on the same row for both suppliers: the specification or grade stated, the packing unit and minimum order quantity, the lead time, freight to your destination port, warranty, documentation and how long the quote stays valid. A unit price that excludes packing or freight is not comparable with one that includes them. Where a supplier proposes a different grade or material, record it as a separate line rather than folding it into the like-for-like price.

Keep a proposed alternative in its own column

If a supplier offers a different reference in place of the one you requested, keep it on its own line. Ask what evidence supports the change and who reviewed it. An alternative does not become acceptable because it sits next to your reference in a catalogue or appears in a supplier photograph. Keep unconfirmed substitutions out of the order list until the specification is confirmed in writing.

Send a line-by-line comparison request

Return one table to every supplier with your reference, vehicle details, required quantity and destination on each line, and ask them to fill the same fields. When the replies come back, place them in a single sheet with the date of each quote, so the price you approve can be traced to the specification you approved. This is the step that turns two different-looking quotes into a decision you can defend.
