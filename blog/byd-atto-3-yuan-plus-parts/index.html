Why the Atto 3 and Yuan Plus name hides a handed-parts problem

BYD sells this platform under more than one name, and buyers use both names in the same enquiry. That is harmless until the order reaches body hardware. A front door latch is not symmetric in the way a bolt is symmetric: the left unit and the right unit are different catalogue lines, the outer handle comes as a left and a right line, and the tailgate lock is its own assembly. A buyer who writes "door handle for Atto 3" has not identified a part; he has identified a choice between two numbers, and possibly between two generations of those two numbers.

The catalogue for this model carries thirty-seven references. Body Parts is the smallest of four groups with seven rows, but it is the group where a handed mistake is most expensive, because the part does not merely fail to fit — it fits in the opposite sense, with the connector, the lever direction or the paint facing the wrong way. Cooling System dominates the table with twenty-one rows, Suspension & Steering has eight, and Filters has one. A buyer can read that distribution as a warning: the high-volume cooling lines are familiar territory, while the small body group is where identification goes wrong.

This guide works from the physical part backwards. Establish which side of the car the piece came from, establish what level of assembly the listing actually describes, and establish which generation of the part is on the vehicle. Only then attach a reference and a quantity. Every number used here is drawn from the catalogue table for this model. No number in this guide is a fitment guarantee; fitment must be confirmed against the VIN or the label on the original part before ordering.

The seven body rows resolve into three handed pairs and one tailgate line. The latch pair is 1316782900, described as the Left Front Door Latch Assembly, and 1316783100, described as the Right Front Door Lock Body Assembly. Notice that the two titles are not worded alike. One says Latch Assembly, the other says Lock Body Assembly. A buyer who assumes that the wording difference is a cataloguing accident has already started down the wrong path; the safe response is to verify the side and the assembly level on the vehicle rather than to infer either from the symmetry of the pair.

The handles form two pairs. 1316842400 is the Left Front Door Outer Handle Assembly and 1316842700 is the Right Front Outer Door Handle Assembly. Separately, 1345119000 and 1345119100 are the left and right front outer handles under a different reference stem. Both pairs describe the same functional part on the same corner of the car, but they are four catalogue lines, not two. The tailgate lock body assembly, 1268094800, sits outside the handed pairs entirely: it has no left or right, but it does have an assembly question of its own — whether the lock body alone is being replaced or the complete tailgate latch with its actuator.

Left, right, and assembly level: the three questions a title cannot answer

A listing title is a convenience, not an identification document. It will tell you that a piece is a handle or a latch; it will rarely tell you which hand it serves, whether the actuator motor is included, or which of the two handle stems belongs to your vehicle. A five-step bench check for a door or tailgate complaint

Body hardware is often replaced on the strength of a symptom alone, and that is how a working actuator gets thrown away with a broken link. The sequence below is deliberately short. It does not require dismantling a door card to prove the point, and it produces the two pieces of evidence the counter actually needs: the side and the assembly level.

  1. Step 1 — name the corner before the part. Write front left, front right or tailgate on the enquiry. Use the vehicle's own orientation, viewed from the driver's seat, and ignore how the part looks when it is lying on a bench.
  2. Step 2 — separate the latch from the actuator. Operate the interior and exterior releases and listen for the actuator. A click with no movement points at the linkage or the lock mechanism; no click at all points at the actuator or its feed. Record what you heard and where.
  3. Step 3 — count the connector cavities. On the original unit, count every cavity in the plug, including empty positions, and note the keying position. Write the observation down as a sentence: for example, "eight cavities, six populated, key at the upper edge". This is a record of what you see, not a published specification.
  4. Step 4 — compare the assembly in your hand with the candidate reference. For 1316782900 and 1316783100, compare the lever direction and mounting flange. For the handle pairs, compare the mount spacing and the clip positions. For 1268094800, confirm whether the motor housing is present.
  5. Step 5 — photograph label and interface in one frame. Put the label, the connector and one mounting point in the same image with a coin or a rule for scale. Then send the side, the observation from step three and the reference together. A buyer who supplies those three things gets one answer instead of three questions.

If the original part cannot be removed before the enquiry, photograph it in position with the door open and state the side explicitly. An in-situ photograph is weaker than a bench photograph, but it is far stronger than a listing title. If the evidence remains conflicting — for example, a reference that points to a right handle but a connector that matches the left — pause the line and ask for clarification. Do not let the conflict be resolved by whoever answers fastest.

Two handle stems: why a same-looking pair is not one order

The clearest example of why titles fail is the pair of handle stems in this catalogue. 1316842400 and 1316842700 are the left and right front outer handles under one numbering stem. 1345119000 and 1345119100 are the left and right front outer handles under another. From a distance, all four are front outer door handles. In a parts system they are four separate lines, and a quotation that collapses them into "handle, pair" has made an assumption about the production period of the vehicle.

The same pattern appears elsewhere in the table. Electric compressor references 1320043000, 1370835100, 1461039100, 1502970200 and 1563684800 are all listed as Electric Compressor Assembly, yet they are distinct lines. Condensers appear under 1319705800 and 1374907600; interior condensers under 1317031300, 1344490800 and 1463215600; front blowers under 1259423700, 1341806700 and 1345307800. None of these repetitions is a duplicate to be quietly merged. Each is a prompt to establish the build date and the interface before quoting. The habit that protects a handle order protects a cooling order too: treat repeated names as a request for more evidence, not as permission to pick the cheapest line.

Market and trim differences that change what you order

The two names for this platform are a clue that specifications travelled. A vehicle sold under one name in one region can carry a different specification from a vehicle sold under the other name elsewhere, and the same name can cover more than one powertrain. For body hardware the consequences are usually mechanical — clip positions, paint finish, lever direction — while for cooling parts they are electrical, since connector keying and voltage rating belong to the build rather than to the marketing name. Record the market, the build date and whether the vehicle is battery-electric or plug-in before comparing any reference.

Duty cycle matters as well. In hot, sun-exposed markets, the cooling lines are the ones that move: the five electric compressors, the two condensers and the three interior condensers all appear repeatedly in enquiries from those regions, while body hardware turns over slowly. In markets where doors are used heavily and vehicles are parked on rough ground, the handles and latches see more cycles, and the handed pairs become the first order rather than an afterthought. These are planning observations rather than measured wear rates; they help decide which lines deserve a first quotation, and they never override the vehicle in front of you.

Pack the handed lines as pairs in the carton plan even when the order quantities are unequal. A shipment with eighteen left handles and two right handles is a valid commercial decision but a poor workshop outcome; label each carton with the side and the full reference so that a warehouse does not have to guess. For high-voltage and safety-related components, ask separately whether the item is a service part, a remanufactured unit or a salvage-derived component, and request the relevant handling documents. These categories are not interchangeable because a catalogue places them under the same vehicle name.

Is 1316842400 the same handle as 1345119000?

They are separate catalogue lines for a front left outer handle. Treat the different stems as a generation or specification signal and confirm against the VIN or the label before ordering. A shared description is not evidence that the mount spacing and clips are the same.

Can the left latch be used on the right door?

Do not assume it can. 1316782900 and 1316783100 are different references, and their titles are not even worded alike. Confirm the side on the vehicle and compare the lever direction and mounting flange with the original part.

Does the tailgate lock body include the actuator motor?

The catalogue lists 1268094800 as a Tailgate Lock Body Assembly. Whether your replacement needs the motor depends on what is fitted and what has failed. Photograph the original and state whether the motor housing and connector are present; the answer is then a matter of record rather than assumption.

Which body reference should a first trial order include?

Start with the corner you can verify: one handed pair with a confirmed stem, and the tailgate line if a vehicle in your-parc has a documented failure. Send quantities per side. Avoid building a first order around a reference chosen from a listing title alone.

Why ask about connector cavities on a mechanical handle?

Because several body components in this range carry electrical releases or actuators, and a connector that looks similar can have a different cavity count or keying. Counting what is physically in front of you is cheap; correcting an electrical mismatch after arrival is not.

Are the thirty-seven references a list of parts available to you?

No. They are an identification list for enquiries. Availability, documentation, lead time and pricing are established for the specific order and destination. Nothing here should be read as a stock or delivery undertaking.
