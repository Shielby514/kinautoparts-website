Why a Coolray order fails at the harness, not at the body shape

The Coolray wears different names in different regions, and the same regional name can cover several build years. A buyer sends a photograph of a door lock body that looks exactly like the one on the shelf, receives a quotation, and finds on installation that the plug will not seat. The casting matched; the connector did not. On this vehicle the most useful identification question is not "what does it look like" but "what is behind the cover, and which build was it fitted to".

Twenty-four references sit in this catalogue, spread across three groups. Body Parts holds eleven, Cooling System nine, and Electrical & Ignition Parts four. The body group is the one that traps buyers: four door lock bodies for four corners, four outer handles in two side-pairs, a tailgate body and two rear door bodies. The electrical group is small but disproportionately important, because two headlamp descriptions are repeated with an assembly suffix and two without it. A title that omits the suffix has omitted the question.

This guide is written for people who have to order across markets. It starts with the build and the region, maps the corner or the subsystem to a reference, then asks for a physical record of the connector before any substitution is accepted. All numbers below come from the catalogue table for this model. None of them is a fitment promise. Fitment must be confirmed against the VIN or the label on the original part before ordering.

Recording a connector: the cavity-count discipline

Harness mismatches are the most common reason a visually correct body part is rejected at fitting. The remedy is not a larger catalogue; it is a repeatable way of describing the plug in front of you. The sequence below takes a few minutes and produces a note that any supplier can act on.

  1. Step 1 — write the build context first. Record the market name as it appears on the vehicle documents, the VIN or plate, the production date and the powertrain. A connector cannot be interpreted without knowing which build it came from.
  2. Step 2 — count cavities, not wires. Count every position in the plug shell, including positions with no wire fitted. A shell with ten cavities and seven populated positions is not the same interface as a seven-cavity shell. Write both numbers down.
  3. Step 3 — note the keying. Describe where the locating tab sits with the plug facing you, and describe any secondary lock. Keying is what prevents a plug from seating even when the cavity count matches.
  4. Step 4 — record wire positions and colours. Number the positions and note the wire in each. You are creating a record of what you observed, not asserting a published wiring specification.
  5. Step 5 — measure on the harness side only. If a circuit check is needed, back-probe the harness side with the appropriate meter and follow the vehicle maker's isolation procedure. Do not probe a module pin or an inflator circuit.
  6. Step 6 — photograph the plug with a scale. Put the plug, the label and a coin or rule in one frame. Then send the build context, the cavity note and the photograph together with the proposed reference.

Example of a usable note: "front right lock body, plug facing me, nine cavities with seven wired, keying at the upper edge, dark green in position two". That sentence is not a specification claim; it is an observation that can be compared with the next part. A note saying "plug looks the same" cannot be compared with anything. Where the evidence conflicts — for instance, a reference that appears correct but a cavity count that differs — keep the line pending and ask for clarification rather than allowing a substitute to be approved for you.

Where market specification changes what sits behind the cover

Regional specification is the reason a body shell can be shared while the parts inside it are not. Lighting regulations, side of drive, marker arrangements and connector standards are set by the market, not by the name on the badge. In this catalogue the clearest example is the lighting group. 7051022400 is the right front combination headlamp and 7051022500 the left front; 7051022800 and 7051022900 are the right and left combination headlamp assemblies. Two descriptions for the same corner, with and without the assembly suffix, mean the buyer must establish what the box is expected to contain — housing only, or housing with the internally mounted components — before comparing prices.

The same logic applies to the cooling lines. Five compressor references from 8013009600 to 8013017000 are all described as air conditioning compressors. A build from one region may use a different connector and a different clutch or drive arrangement from a build in another. Condensers 8010075300 and 8010572500 need the port positions checked rather than assumed from the photograph. In hot markets, the cooling lines generate most enquiries; in markets with heavy door use, the four-corner lock bodies and the two handle pairs do. Those are planning patterns, not measured wear rates, and they never replace the build evidence on the specific vehicle.

Is 1018061055 a front or a rear lock body?

It is listed as the left front door lock body. The rear left is 1018061057, rear right 1018061058 and front right 1018061056. Confirm the corner on the vehicle and the connector record before ordering, because the castings can look very similar.

Why are there two right front handles in the list?

5077039100 and 5077039200 are both described as right front outer opening handles, with 5077039300 and 5077039400 on the left. Two lines for one corner signal a variant. Compare clip positions, finish and plug shape against the original part rather than choosing on price.

What is the practical difference between the two headlamp descriptions?

7051022400 and 7051022500 are listed as combination headlamps, while 7051022800 and 7051022900 are listed as combination headlamp assemblies. Ask for a written description of what each box contains and confirm against the VIN and the lens photograph before accepting either.

Do I need to count empty cavities in a plug?

Yes. The shell determines whether a plug can seat; the populated positions determine what the circuit can do. Record both numbers, the keying position and the wire positions as observations. This is the most useful single piece of evidence you can send with a body order.

Can a lock body from another market be used if the casting matches?

Not on the casting alone. Regional harness keying and connector arrangements can differ within the same shell. Confirm against the VIN or label and compare the connector record before accepting a substitution.

Are these twenty-four references a list of parts you can supply immediately?

No. The table is an identification aid for enquiries. Availability, documentation, lead time and pricing are established for the specific order and destination. Nothing in this guide is a stock or delivery undertaking.
